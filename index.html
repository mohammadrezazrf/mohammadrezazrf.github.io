Major Projects

Endoscopic Capsule Robots

This project focuses on the design, prototyping, and control of magnetically actuated capsule endoscopes. A major part of this research involved the design and experimental validation of a biopsy mechanism for capsule robots. Since both capsule localization and biopsy actuation rely on magnetic fields, the design aimed to minimize interference between these functions while maintaining a fail-safe biopsy mechanism. In addition, I investigated optimal control strategies for capsule robots to minimize the energy consumption of the actuation coils. This project was part of my M.Sc. thesis at Sharif University of Technology and resulted in two publications. The video below demonstrates the experimental position control of the developed capsule robot.

Magnetic Localization Using Deep Neural Networks

Magnetic tracking offers a promising alternative to fluoroscopic imaging for localizing intrabody medical devices. Conventional approaches combine mathematical magnetic field models with iterative algorithms, but they are often computationally intensive, sensitive to initial estimates, and prone to divergence. Although artificial neural networks can overcome some of these limitations, they typically require large training datasets, which can be challenging to obtain in clinical settings. In this project, I developed a CNN-based localization method that incorporates computer vision techniques to improve localization performance while reducing reliance on extensive training data. This project was part of my M.Sc. thesis at Sharif University of Technology and resulted in a journal publication.

Magnetic Manipulation of Ferrofluid Marble Robots

My research in this area focuses on the magnetic manipulation and control of ferrofluid marble robots. I have contributed to two research projects involving the experimental validation of control strategies under different operating conditions. These include position control of a single robot in the presence of model uncertainties and independent control of two robots operating in shared and parallel workspaces.

This research was conducted voluntarily at Sharif University of Technology and has resulted in two manuscripts currently in preparation. The video below demonstrates the independent control of two ferrofluid marble robots in parallel workspaces using substrate fluids with different viscosities.

Other Projects

Steel Surface Defect Detection and Classification Using Semantic Segmentation

This project was completed as part of my undergraduate Computer Vision course. I investigated semantic segmentation algorithms, including FCN-8 and U-Net with different backbone architectures, for detecting and classifying defects on steel surfaces. The project resulted in a conference paper.

Ball and Obstacle Game Simulator in C++

This project was developed as part of my undergraduate Computer Programming course. The objective was to implement a ball-and-obstacle game simulator in C++ using the `graphics.h` library, with separate classes to represent the ball and generate the obstacles.

Object Detection Using YOLOv5

This project was developed as part of my undergraduate Artificial Neural Networks course. The objective was to use YOLO models to detect bicycles, motorbikes, and their riders, and determine whether the riders were wearing helmets. The dataset was created by manually annotating images from the Helmet Detection dataset. The validation was perfomed on a random video from YouTube.

Human Action Recognition

This project was developed as part of my undergraduate Artificial Neural Networks course. The objective was to recognize six types of human activities (walking, jogging, running, boxing, hand waving, and hand clapping) from videos using the KTH dataset. I implemented a combination of CNNs and LSTM networks to build the model. The model was evaluated using several self-recorded videos.

Fabrication of a Micromixer for Mixing Rhodamine B in Water

This project was developed as part of my graduate Lab-on-a-Chip course. The objective was to design and fabricate a microfluidic device to mix Rhodamine B powder with a limited volume of water. The device geometry was designed to promote powder dissolution as the water droplet flowed through the microchannels. The prototype was fabricated from Plexiglas using laser cutting.
